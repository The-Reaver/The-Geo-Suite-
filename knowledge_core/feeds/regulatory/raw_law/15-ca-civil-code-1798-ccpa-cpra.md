# Cal. Civil Code §1798.100 — CCPA/CPRA

- **Jurisdiction:** California
- **Domain:** Patient data privacy (website/consumer data, distinct from clinical medical records)
- **Source:** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-15
- **Retrieval method:** Direct fetch, verbatim (excerpt)
- **Verification status:** Not yet lawyer-reviewed

## Text

**Core obligation:**
> "A business that controls the collection of a consumer's personal information shall, at or before
> the point of collection, inform consumers..."

**Required disclosures** (each item's quote reformatted 2026-08-16 so it starts on its own
blockquote line — the original inline "1. >" numbered-list format caused the raw-to-notes converter
to split each quote at its own line break, capturing only the orphaned continuation half as a
fragment note; fixed here at the source rather than patched downstream):

Item 1:
> "The categories of personal information to be collected and the purposes for which the categories of personal information are collected or used..."

Item 2, if applicable:
> "the categories of sensitive personal information to be collected and the purposes for which the categories of sensitive personal information are collected or used..."

Item 3:
> "The length of time the business intends to retain each category of personal information..."

Item 4, third-party sharing agreements must specify personal information is:
> "sold or disclosed by the business only for limited and specified purposes."

Item 5:
> "implement reasonable security procedures and practices appropriate to the nature of the personal information..."

Amended by Proposition 24 (CPRA), operative January 1, 2023.

## Why this is here

Governs the marketing website itself — the intake/contact forms on both Paradise Hyperbarics' and
Hyperbaric Lab's sites collect personal (and likely sensitive/health-adjacent) information, which
brings CCPA/CPRA's disclosure obligations into play independent of CMIA (file 14, which covers
clinical medical records specifically) or HIPAA (federal file 06).
