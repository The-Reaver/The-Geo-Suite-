# 45 CFR §164.502 — HIPAA Privacy Rule, General Uses and Disclosures

- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Domain:** Patient data privacy
- **Source:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.502
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-15
- **Retrieval method:** Direct fetch, verbatim (key subsections; full section is long, see scope note)
- **Verification status:** Not yet lawyer-reviewed
- **Scope note:** 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164 together are the full HIPAA Privacy/Security Rule — this

## Text

**§164.502(a) — Standard:**
> "A covered entity or business associate may not use or disclose protected health information,
> except as permitted or required by this subpart or by subpart C of part 160."

**§164.502(a)(1) — Covered Entities: Permitted Uses (paraphrased list of grounds, not exhaustive
verbatim text — flag for direct re-check):**
- "To the individual"
- "For treatment, payment, or health care operations"
- "Incident to a use or disclosure otherwise permitted or required"
- Pursuant to valid authorization
- Pursuant to agreements under §164.510
- As permitted by §164.512, §164.514(e)–(g)

**§164.502(b) — Minimum Necessary:**
> "A covered entity or business associate must make reasonable efforts to limit protected health
> information to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose of the use, disclosure, or
> request."

**§164.502(a)(5) — Prohibited Uses:**
- Health plans cannot "use or disclose protected health information that is genetic information for
  underwriting purposes."
- Covered entities cannot "sell protected health information" except under specific circumstances.
- Prohibition on using PHI "to conduct a criminal, civil, or administrative investigation into any
  person for the mere act of seeking, obtaining, providing, or facilitating reproductive health care."

## Why this is here

The baseline federal patient-privacy standard — needed as the comparison point even if it turns out
neither demo business is a HIPAA covered entity, since the audit report should be explicit about which
regime actually applies rather than assuming HIPAA by default.
