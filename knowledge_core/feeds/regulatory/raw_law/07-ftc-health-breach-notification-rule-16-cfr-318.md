# 16 CFR Part 318 — FTC Health Breach Notification Rule

- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Domain:** Patient data privacy — likely the more directly applicable federal rule than HIPAA for a
  cash-pay HBOT marketing site with an online intake form (see the open question flagged in file 06)
- **Source:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/318.3
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-15
- **Retrieval method:** Direct fetch, but the source page itself did not contain standalone verbatim
  definitions of "breach of security" / "personal health record" — only the operative notification
  requirements below, plus references to definitions held elsewhere in Part 318. **Flag: pull §318.2
  (definitions) directly next pass.**
- **Verification status:** Not yet lawyer-reviewed

## Text

**Notification requirements (§318.3):**
> "Notify each individual who is a citizen or resident of the United States whose unsecured PHR
> identifiable health information was acquired by an unauthorized person"

> "Notify the Federal Trade Commission"

> "Notify prominent media outlets serving a State or jurisdiction" if 500+ residents' information was
> breached.

**Discovery standard:**
> "A breach of security shall be treated as discovered as of the first day on which such breach is
> known or reasonably should have been known to the vendor"

**Who this applies to (per the rule's own scope, not verified verbatim this pass):** vendors of
personal health records and PHR-related entities — a category that can include a health/wellness
business's website even when the business itself isn't a HIPAA covered entity.

## Why this is here

If Paradise Hyperbarics or Hyperbaric Lab collect health information through an online form and aren't
HIPAA covered entities, this FTC rule — not HIPAA — is likely the operative federal patient-data
obligation. Needs §318.2's actual definitions pulled next to confirm scope precisely.
