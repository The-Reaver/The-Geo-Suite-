# 21 CFR §801.5 — FDA Device Labeling, Adequate Directions for Use

- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Domain:** Medical marketing / device claims
- **Source:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/801.5
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-15
- **Retrieval method:** Direct fetch, verbatim
- **Verification status:** Not yet lawyer-reviewed
- **Scope note:** 21 CFR Part 801 is a large part; this file captures §801.5 specifically (the
  "adequate directions for use" / intended-use provision, the section most relevant to marketing-claim
  scope). It does not cover the full Part 801 — additional sections (e.g. §801.109, prescription

## Text

**Opening definition:**
> "Adequate directions for use means directions under which the layman can use a device safely and for
> the purposes for which it is intended."

**The regulation requires directions to specify:**

(a):
> "Statements of all conditions, purposes, or uses for which such device is intended, including
> conditions, purposes, or uses for which it is prescribed, recommended, or suggested in its oral,
> written, printed, or graphic advertising..."

(b):
> "Quantity of dose, including usual quantities for each of the uses for which it is intended and
> usual quantities for persons of different ages and different physical conditions."

(c):
> "Frequency of administration or application."

(d):
> "Duration of administration or application."

(e):
> "Time of administration or application, in relation to time of meals, time of onset of symptoms, or
> other time factors."

(f):
> "Route or method of administration or application."

(g):
> "Preparation for use, i.e., adjustment of temperature, or other manipulation or process."

## Why this is here

Subsection (a) is the key hook: FDA "intended use" is determined in part by what a device is
"prescribed, recommended, or suggested" for in its advertising — meaning a marketing claim itself can
expand a device's regulatory intended use beyond its cleared indications. Directly relevant to
auditing an HBOT clinic's marketing copy.
