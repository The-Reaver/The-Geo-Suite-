## Text — the complete list of FDA-cleared HBOT indications

> "The FDA has cleared hyperbaric oxygen therapy for treatment in these disorders:
> - air and gas bubbles in blood vessels
> - anemia (severe anemia when blood transfusions cannot be used)
> - burns (severe and large burns treated at a specialized burn center)
> - carbon monoxide poisoning
> - crush injury
> - decompression sickness (diving risk)
> - gas gangrene
> - hearing loss (complete hearing loss that occurs suddenly and without any known cause)
> - infection of the skin and bone (severe)
> - radiation injury
> - skin graft flap at risk of tissue death
> - vision loss (when sudden and painless in one eye due to blockage of blood flow)
> - wounds (non-healing, diabetic foot ulcers)"

**This is the exact, complete list neither demo business's marketing can claim beyond without
crossing into unapproved/off-label territory** — the single most directly applicable yardstick in
this entire raw law set for auditing an HBOT clinic's website.

## Text — the explicit off-label warning

> "The FDA is aware there are some hyperbaric oxygen treatment centers promoting hyperbaric oxygen
> chambers for uses that have not been cleared or approved by the FDA, such as treatment of cancer,
> Lyme disease, autism, or Alzheimer's disease."

> "If your health care provider recommends HBOT, the FDA advises you get the treatment at a hospital
> or facility that has been inspected and is accredited by the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical
> Society."

> HBO2 treatment "is being studied for other conditions, including COVID-19, but has not cleared or
> authorized the use of any hyperbaric device to treat COVID-19 or any conditions beyond those listed
> above."

## Text — device safety recommendations (August 25, 2025 letter, current)

> "The FDA is aware of reports of serious injuries and deaths with use of HBOT devices and is
> providing recommendations for health care providers and facilities to help reduce potential
> risks."

Recommendations include: reviewing manufacturer instructions for each device, fire-prevention and
grounding measures ("heightened risk of fire with use of oxygen at a high concentration"), staff
training, patient monitoring, manufacturer-specified cleaning/maintenance schedules, avoiding
prohibited items (electrical/static devices), and patient clothing of "hyperbaric compatible
materials" (e.g. cotton, since "wool and synthetic materials" may produce more static electricity).

**Regulatory classification, confirmed:** "HBOT devices are Class II medical devices and are
cleared by the FDA through the 510(k) process," identifiable by Product Code CBF in the FDA's
510(k) database.

Real-world grounding: the letter cites actual, recent reports of fires causing "serious injuries and
deaths" with HBOT devices, root cause not yet determined as of the letter's issuance — this is not
a hypothetical risk being described.
